About Me
I am a Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) researcher, system designer, and educator specializing in Green AI, Green Computer Vision, and More-than-Human (MtH) interaction design. Holding a Ph.D. in Digital & Analytical Sciences from the University of Salzburg, my research bridges digital transformation with ecological responsibility, focusing on how intelligent computational systems can function within global planetary boundaries.


Currently serving as a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Institute for Computational Medicine (Vetmeduni Vienna), I design energy-efficient computer vision systems, hardware sensors, and sustainable precision livestock technologies deployed in real-world settings. My work combines empirical user research, physical computing, and socio-ecological impact assessment to move interactive systems beyond traditional screens and foster meaningful well-being for human, non-human, and environmental actors.
Core Research Focus
Green AI & Energy-Efficient Vision: Optimizing real-time computer vision pipelines for resource-constrained edge devices and low-energy environments.
More-than-Human Interaction: Designing sociotechnical interfaces that incorporate non-human agents, animals, and broader ecological context.
Precision Animal Health & Sensors: Developing non-invasive, sensor-based technologies for livestock care and field-based observation.
In-situ & Ethnographic HCI: Utilizing 360° observation, situated action frameworks, and participatory design to evaluate technology in natural settings.
